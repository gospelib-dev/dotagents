# Room playbooks: daily-life rooms

Tags as in SKILL.md. Section numbers continue the original numbering.

## 1. Kitchen

**Steps:** trash/expired -> duplicates and unused gadgets -> relocate non-kitchen items -> zones -> containers.
**Zones:** prep (knives, boards, mixing bowls near the main counter); cook (pots, oils, spices near the stove); clean-up (sponges, soap, towels at the sink); serve/eat (dishes near dishwasher); bake; kids' snack/cup zone at child height.
**Rules:** daily-use items between knee and shoulder; once-a-year items go to garage or basement high shelves; a counter holds only what is used daily.
**Time:** 4-8 h (judgment). **Goes wrong:** buying organizers before culling; storing the "good dishes" in the most accessible cabinet; spices above the stove (heat). **Maintenance:** weekly fridge and counter reset; quarterly cabinet glance.
**Safety:** freestanding ranges need anti-tip brackets [CPSC]; keep cleaners/medicines/matches/knives in latched or high cabinets, and call Poison Help 1-800-222-1222 if swallowed [CPSC]; keep a 3-ft kid-free zone from hot things [NFPA]; plug major appliances directly into the wall, no extension cords [NFPA]; keep cooking areas clear of towels and paper.

## 2. Pantry and food storage

**Steps:** pull everything -> toss expired/damaged cans and stale items -> group by category -> measure shelves -> decant only items you use fast.
**Zones:** breakfast, baking, canned goods, snacks (child-reachable lower shelf), bulk backstock (top), small-appliance shelf.
**Facts:** keep dry goods cool, dry and dark; the extension guide lists flour in an airtight container, 6-8 months refrigerated, 12 months frozen, and ground spices about 6 months; refrigerator 35-40 F, freezer 0 F or below; avoid damaged cans; date and rotate. [EXT] An extension search snippet (not the opened page) also advised shelves 2-3 ft off the floor for stored food; treat as (judgment) for a basement pantry. Product dates are not federal requirements except infant formula; they are only reliable if the food was kept at proper temperature. [EXT]
**Basement pantry rule:** use closed plastic bins on shelving, never on the floor; check humidity (target below 60 percent, ideally 30-50). [EPA]
**Time:** 2-3 h. **Wrong:** bulk-buy backstock without space; decanting everything (cooking instructions lost). **Maintenance:** FIFO rotation: new at back; quarterly 15-minute date check.

## 3. Closets (bedroom, hall, coat)

**Steps:** empty fully -> try on or ask "worn in the last 12 months?" -> sort by type -> hang vs fold -> add shelves/hooks.
**Adult closet:** hang what wrinkles; fold knits; seasonal swap boxes on the top shelf. **Kid closet:** low rod and hooks so the child can dress alone; one bin "outgrown" that is emptied monthly (donate or hand-down). **Hall/coat closet:** one coat per hook per person; boots on a tray.
**Time:** 1-2 h per closet. **Wrong:** keeping "someday" sizes; double-rod too high for kids; overloaded wire shelves sagging.
**Shelf loads:** ratings depend on installation. Manufacturer listings (retail pages, search snippet only, not opened) gave about 15 lb per ft for fixed-mount wire shelves and 25 lb/ft (44 lb/ft into studs) for adjustable track; always check the sheet in your kit and anchor into studs, spread weight evenly. (unverified; check your kit)
**Maintenance:** 5-minute "one in, one out" at laundry folding; seasonal swap in October and April.

## 4. Kids' rooms

**Steps (child present, 3-5 h):** safety first -> clothes -> books -> toys (section 5) -> desk/homework -> decor.
**Zones:** sleep (bed clear underneath, nothing on top of dressers), dress (low rod/drawers), play (floor mat plus open bins), read (basket and light), homework (desk and supplies), display (shelf for art, a "rotating" frame).
**Safety (all from CPSC unless noted):**
- Anchor dressers, bookcases and TVs to the wall or floor; install the anti-tip kit that came with new furniture; keep toys and remotes off furniture tops so children are not tempted to climb. Most anti-tip kits cost under $20 and install in under 20 minutes (CPSC campaign release found via search; not opened). Supervision is still advised even with anchoring.
- Cordless window coverings; if you cannot replace, tension devices and short pull cords. Keep window openings at 4 inches or less where children can reach, but keep at least one window per room usable for fire escape.
- Button/coin batteries: keep remotes and devices without screw closures out of reach; if swallowed seek medical help immediately, National Battery Ingestion Hotline 1-800-498-8666 or Poison Help 1-800-222-1222.
- Two ways out of every sleeping room and children taught to open windows, remove screens, unlock doors. [NFPA] Smoke and CO alarms in every sleeping room and on every level incl. basement. [NFPA]
**Wrong:** one giant toy box; no home for "projects in progress"; sibling shared rooms without per-child bins. **Maintenance:** Friday 10-minute tidy-song; quarterly outgrown purge; birthday/holiday "in-out" before gifts arrive.

## 5. Toys, games and craft

**Steps:** find all toys house-wide -> remove broken, missing-pieces, recalled -> keep by category (building, pretend, vehicles, art, games, puzzles) -> rotate.
**Method:** keep roughly a third visible; store the rest in opaque bins labeled by category and rotate every 4-8 weeks (judgment). Puzzles/games: one zip bag per puzzle, rubber band games shut, tape the contents list inside.
**What goes wrong:** donating incomplete sets (charities refuse them; charities commonly require all pieces [charity guidance]), saving "special" items with no display plan.
**Handle with care:** magnets and button-battery toys; do not pass on recalled, banned or non-compliant items (charity donation guidance states this; check CPSC recalls before gifting).
**Maintenance:** one-in-one-out; kids choose the "going" bin with a parent present. **Time:** 2-3 h.

## 6. Bathrooms

**Steps:** expired/unused products -> duplicates -> zones (daily, grooming, first aid, cleaning) -> towel plan.
**Medicine:** store out of reach and latched; dispose of unused meds via take-back (pharmacy or DEA collector) or mail-back; if none, FDA's trash method: mix with coffee grounds or cat litter, seal, throw away, scratch out labels; flush only items on FDA's flush list. [FDA]
**Safety:** water heater at 120 F or lower and anti-scald devices [CPSC]; nonslip mats; never leave babies unattended in the bath [CPSC].
**Moisture:** run the fan; keep humidity below 60 percent [EPA]. **Time:** 1-2 h. **Maintenance:** monthly 10-minute expiry check; replace towels yearly.

## 7. Linen closet and bedding

**Steps:** pull all -> keep two sets of sheets per bed plus one spare, 2-3 towels per person -> donate or move extras -> fold-and-stack by room or bed size.
**Trick:** store each sheet set inside one of its own pillowcases. Old towels/blankets: animal shelters or rag use (confirm with the shelter; judgment).
**Time:** 45-90 min. **Wrong:** storing in a damp basement; plastic bins without airflow for natural fibers. **Maintenance:** swap-out at each laundry day.

## 8. Laundry room

**Steps:** clear surfaces -> chemicals up and away (latched) -> sorting baskets -> hang rod/shelf above the machines.
**Safety [NFPA]:** clean the lint filter before each load; clean the vent pipe every 3 months; use rigid or flexible metal duct; do not run the dryer when you leave home or sleep; keep gas dryers professionally inspected yearly; air-dry fabrics exposed to flammable cleaners; keep 3 ft clear of combustibles around furnace/dryer. Oily/solvent rags can ignite on their own: store in a sealed metal container or hang outside to dry first. [NFPA]
**Time:** 1-2 h. **Maintenance:** quarterly vent clean; empty lint screen every load.

## 9. Entryway / drop zone

**Steps:** measure the wall -> one hook, one cubby or one bin per person -> shoe tray -> "outbox" for returns -> command center (calendar, keys, school forms).
**Rules:** the zone holds only the current season; the inbox for paper is a single tray that gets processed weekly (see `household-paper-flow`). Paper from school goes to the tray, never the counter.
**Safety:** keep the front exit and path clear [NFPA]; no cords across doorways [NFPA].
**Time:** 1-2 h plus install. **Wrong:** too many hooks hung too high for kids. **Maintenance:** daily 2-minute shoe reset; seasonal swap.

## 10. Home office

**Steps:** paper triage into 5 stacks (action, file, shred, recycle, scan) -> clear desk -> cable management -> supplies cull -> filing (see `household-paper-flow`) -> equipment disposal (`unwanted-item-routing`).
**Zones:** active (current work), reference (one drawer), supplies, tech, archive (offsite shelf). Aim for a desk surface holding only the monitor, task lamp and one in-tray.
**Safety [NFPA]:** surge protectors and power strips only with overload protection and testing-lab listing; extension cords are temporary, never under rugs or across doorways; replace cracked cords; keep lamps away from anything that can burn; call an electrician if breakers trip repeatedly. Charge laptops, phones and power banks on a hard flat surface, not a bed, pillow or couch; unplug when full. [NFPA]
**Time:** 4-8 h plus the paper backlog (see sibling). **Wrong:** paper piles with no inbox; cables as the "last step". **Maintenance:** weekly 15-minute paper processing; monthly backup check.

**When every cabinet is already full (storage-map pattern):** a high-level overview gets rejected as vague. Deliver instead: (1) one theme per cabinet, with a table per cabinet (zone, height, category, container); (2) shelf heights from the inside floor, with assumptions stated; (3) a phased shopping list (phase 1 = only what the first cabinet needs; the rest after the sort, after measuring); (4) a domino sequence: first move out what leaves the room, to free a shelf, then empty ONE cabinet at a time into labeled "goes-to" boxes (reuse the cardboard already on the floor) and leave a deliberately empty reserve zone; (5) never invent current contents: ask one short text question listing each cabinet ("2-4 words: what's mostly in it?") plus the 2-3 measurements that gate purchases.
