# Retention details, tax archive and child identity

## 2. What to keep, and for how long (details)

### 2.1 Taxes [IRS How long should I keep records]
- **3 years** from filing: standard window for supporting records.
- **3 years from filing or 2 years from payment, whichever is later**, if you file a claim for credit or refund.
- **6 years** if you left out income above 25 percent of gross income shown.
- **7 years** for a worthless-securities or bad-debt deduction.
- **4 years** for employment-tax records (household employer, nanny; counted from when the tax was due or paid, whichever is later).
- **Indefinitely** if you did not file or filed a fraudulent return.
- **Property:** keep records until the limit expires for the year you dispose of the property, because you need them to compute gain/loss and depreciation (home purchase, major improvements, vehicle for business use).
- Keep copies of filed returns. Before discarding records, check whether an insurer, creditor or other party needs them longer. [IRS]
- Home improvement receipts: keep until you sell the home since they may reduce capital gains. [FTC 2015]
- **Charitable giving records [IRS Topic 506, Pub 526]:** any cash gift needs a bank record or charity note; $250 or more needs a written acknowledgment from the charity; noncash over $500 needs Form 8283; over $5,000 per item/group needs a qualified appraisal; used clothing and household items must be in good used condition or better to deduct (Pub 526); for tax year 2026, non-itemizers may deduct up to $1,000 ($2,000 joint) of qualifying cash gifts (Topic 506; verify current law with your preparer). Keep every donation receipt with the tax return.
- **Online selling [IRS 1099-K FAQ]:** marketplaces report only if gross payments exceed $20,000 and 200 transactions (federal; a state may be lower); payments are taxable unless the law says otherwise, whether or not you get a form; you subtract fees, shipping and refunds; basis is in Pub 551. IRS search summary (not opened as a page): a loss on a personal item is not deductible but not taxable income either; keep the original purchase info if you sell something at a gain.

### 2.2 Credit and banking
- Negative credit info generally stays on reports 7 years; bankruptcies up to 10 years. [CFPB]
- CFPB retention rules in the results governed institutions, not households; the CFPB did not publish a household retention schedule in what I opened. Follow IRS and FTC lists.
- Banks keep check copies for 7 years under state law (CFPB search snippet; not opened), so you can request old checks rather than hoard them.

### 2.3 Home, vehicles, insurance, medical (judgment unless tagged)
- **Home:** deed, title policy, closing papers, improvements: until 7 years after sale or longer. Warranties/manuals: while you own the item, or digital only. [FTC]
- **Insurance:** current policy and declarations pages; claim records 3-7 years; life and long-term-care policies: permanent. Flood: standard homeowners insurance typically does not cover flooding; NFIP is the federal option. [ready.gov]
- **Medical:** keep vital health records permanently, especially older than electronic health records (FTC). Itemized bills and EOBs: until disputes resolve, about a year if no issue (FTC). Immunization records forever, including every child's.
- **Children:** birth certificate and Social Security card locked; school records, report cards, IEP/504 plans (keep long term; judgment); freeze each child's credit under 16 (FTC; see "Child identity protection" below).
- **Employment and benefits:** final pay stubs, W-2 summaries, retirement statements permanent.
- **Estate:** wills, powers of attorney, health directives, beneficiary designations: originals locked, copies shared with named agents; review after any marriage, birth, move or death. State-specific forms and filing steps vary; ask an estate attorney.

## 3. Annual tax-year archive routine (30 minutes each January)

1. Create a box or digital folder named "Tax Year 20XX" with the return, W-2s/1099s, key receipts, donation receipts.
2. Label with the destroy-after year (return year + 7).
3. Pull the box from 7 years ago; shred after a quick check that nothing is a property record, loan document or active-claim item.
4. Keep the return PDF indefinitely.


## 5. Child identity protection [FTC]
- Ask why a school or provider needs a child's SSN, how it is protected, and whether the last four digits suffice.
- Lock documents with children's information; shred paper before discarding; community shred day if no shredder.
- Parents may request a free credit freeze for a child under 16 at each of the three bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion); it stays until you remove it; a lift must happen within an hour; report misuse at IdentityTheft.gov. Have proof of authority, such as the child's birth certificate and SSN card, and your ID ready.
