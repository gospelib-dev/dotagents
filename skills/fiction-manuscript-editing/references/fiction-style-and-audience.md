# Fiction style, Chicago baseline, and YA lens

## Editorial levels

Editors Canada separates structural, stylistic, copy, and proofreading stages while acknowledging overlap and varying terminology [S1]. EFA similarly distinguishes developmental work on content/organization/genre, line work on sentence/paragraph style, and copyediting for grammar, usage, punctuation, cross-references, and consistency [S2].

Typical mapping for a novel:

- **Developmental:** plot, arc, scene purpose, causality, agency, pacing, outline function.
- **Line/stylistic:** clarity, coherence, rhythm, imagery, register, POV texture, repetition.
- **Copy:** grammar, punctuation, spelling, dialogue mechanics, names, timeline, style sheet.
- **Proof:** final introduced errors and formatting; not a new developmental pass.

## Fiction style sheet

Track at minimum [S1, S5, S7]:

- US spelling and punctuation;
- names, forms of address, capitalization, pronouns, ages, appearance;
- invented terms, technology, ships, titles, italics, hyphenation;
- dialogue, thoughts, telepathy, recordings, translated speech;
- number treatment and technical readouts;
- POV and tense by scene;
- location, timeline, injuries, inventory, knowledge, entrances/exits;
- plot promises and unresolved threads.

The style sheet records choices; it does not override canon or binding locks.

## Chicago fiction essentials

Use _The Chicago Manual of Style_, 18th ed., as the baseline and Amy J. Schneider's fiction-specific companion where general CMOS guidance is insufficient [S6, S7].

1. **Dialogue tags:** speech verbs can attach to quoted dialogue; physical actions generally require their own sentence or a recast. Do not make a character “smile” or “point” words [S6].
2. **Interruption:** use an em dash for cut-off or interruption [S6].
3. **Trailing/faltering:** use a three-point ellipsis for speech that trails or falters; four dots imply an omission/period context, not ordinary fictional trailing speech [S6].
4. **House consistency:** preserve the manuscript's established quotation, italics, thought, and technical-display conventions unless the brief changes them.
5. **Purposeful exception:** fragments, dialect, and nonstandard syntax may carry voice or pace. Editors Canada explicitly recognizes fiction exceptions in copyediting [S1].
6. **Query threshold:** query what the editor cannot resolve or what changes meaning/intent; make routine corrections directly [S1, S4].

## YA lens

YALSA's eligibility range is 12–18 and spans broad reading and maturity levels [S9]. Do not convert that into one grade score.

Check:

- Does a young protagonist make consequential choices?
- Are stakes immediate in the character's life, not only in world lore?
- Can a reader follow who knows what, why a choice happens, and what it costs?
- Does specialized vocabulary arrive with usable context?
- Does emotion appear through decision, body, speech, or relationship before explanation repeats it?
- Are adult/AI/technology solutions enabling the protagonist's choice rather than replacing it?
- Is the conflict something the teen characters care about and act on [S10]?

Readability metrics can reveal sentence-density outliers, but the pass/fail test is contextual comprehension, engagement, and age-appropriate agency.

## Actionable editorial communication

Professional notes are collaborative rather than adversarial [S8, S16].

- Start with the strongest working benchmark so revision has a model [S14].
- Describe what is on the page before inferring intent.
- Name the reader effect: confusion, delay, flattened escalation, lost voice, or broken continuity.
- Explain why it matters to this scene/book/audience.
- Specify the required outcome; label examples as options.
- Ask an open question when intent is unclear instead of imposing one solution [S14].
- Keep queries clear, tactful, succinct, and actionable [S1].
- Prioritize a short set of high-leverage classes over a flood of unranked line notes.

A developmental letter addresses big-picture strengths/issues; inline examples make abstract concepts concrete [S12, S15].
