# Decluttering method guide

## Purpose
Pick the right decluttering method for the situation, sequence the work, design sessions, apply
consistent keep / donate / sell / discard criteria, and hand over maintenance that survives.
No trial shows any named method beats another; matching method to situation is professional logic.

Tags: [research] = peer-reviewed or formal study; [professional practice] = what organizer
bodies/practitioners describe; [popular claim] = book/blog assertion, untested. Read "Honest
limits" before quoting any science to the family.

## When to use
- Advising on decluttering or organizing a home, a room or a category.
- Choosing session length, room order, or a keep/let-go rule.
- Planning maintenance after a reset.

## Method

## 1. Decision guide (use this first)

Step 0 - Safety and scope screen. Ask or observe before choosing any method:
- Paths blocked, no usable bed/stove/sink, pests, mold, or fire risk, or the person cannot
  discard without acute distress (Clutter-Hoarding Scale 3+ territory)? STOP the method
  menu. Use the harm-reduction guidance in `references/time-evidence-and-limits.md` (refer to a clinician/CD-trained organizer).
- Whose stuff is it? Never discard another adult's things without their say. Kids get
  age-scaled choice. Shared spaces need a household agreement first (section 4, facilitation rules).

Step 1 - Pick by situation (first match wins):
| Situation | Method | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Overwhelmed, stalls on decisions, "decluttering paralysis" | Dana White no-mess + trash sweep first (3.2) | final decision per item, visible wins, no pile limbo |
| Deadline (move, guests, sale) under 4 weeks | Room-by-room, Four-Box, timer (3.8), sell/donate booked in advance | speed; defer sentimental |
| High energy, wants one big reset, mostly decides fast | KonMari category order (3.1) | one pass, category gathering shows true volume |
| Low energy, ADHD-type, tired parents | FlyLady-style 15-minute timer + zones (3.6), body double | small, repeatable, no big-event dependency |
| Room feels wrong but nothing is "junk"; function problem | Morgenstern theme + SPACE (3.3) | fixes layout and homes, not just volume |
| Shared room with couple/partner conflict | Walsh vision-first (3.4) | agree on the purpose before any purge |
| Older adult, inherited volume, legacy concern | Death cleaning (3.5) | gift with story, sentimental last |
| Wants to test whether they need things | 90/90, 20/20, packing party (3.7) | trial removal, reversible |
| Kids' toys, collections, wardrobe overflow | Container concept (3.2) | capacity cap ends negotiation per item |
| Already decluttered, keeps refilling | One-in-one-out + weekly reset (3.9, section 5) | intake control |

Step 2 - Order of work (default when information is thin):
1. Trash, recycling, obvious duplicates and expired items whole-house sweep (30-60 min).
2. Pick ONE high-use, low-emotion zone (kitchen counter, entry, one closet). Finish it fully.
3. Then bedrooms/clothes, bathrooms, kids' spaces, shared living areas.
4. Paper and sentimental last (decisions are hardest; skill is built by then).
5. Garage/basement/storage: schedule as its own multi-session project with a dump/donation run.
6. Containers last: buy nothing until the keep set is known.

Step 3 - Session design defaults: 90-120 min working blocks with a 10-min break (3-hour
minimums are common in paid practice, 5 hours for first sessions [professional practice]),
a timer, a donation bag at the door, one stop point that leaves the room usable. Tired
household: 15-30 minutes. Always end by removing outbound items from the house the same day.

Step 4 - Default keep/let-go rule when unsure: keep if used in the last 12 months or it
has an assigned home that fits; let go if broken, duplicate beyond the container cap, or
replaceable cheaply (section 3). Unsure -> "maybe box," dated, taped, shelved elsewhere;
unopened in 90 days -> go. Sentimental -> deferred to its own session, cap by container.

Step 5 - Close every session: photo of the result, list what left the house, name the next
session's target and time, set one small maintenance rule.

## 2. How to run the engagement (the agent's workflow)

1. Intake (15-30 min, or a questionnaire): goals in the family's words, who lives here,
   rooms ranked by pain, deadlines, energy/health/time per week, past attempts and what
   failed, safety flags, budget, who must agree. Ask for before-photos.
2. Choose the method with section 1; state it plainly and why.
3. Make a plan: rooms in order, sessions with times, outbound logistics, a done-definition
   per zone ("floor clear, every item has a home, nothing on the counter that lives elsewhere").
4. Run sessions via the section 4 loop.
5. Review each week: what left, what is next, what blocked progress.
6. Hand over maintenance (section 5) and teach the household why, so it survives without you.

Method details (KonMari, Dana K. White container concept, Morgenstern SPACE, Walsh vision-first,
death cleaning, FlyLady, Minimalists 90/90 and 20/20, Four-Box, one-in-one-out, Home Edit,
danshari), with best fit, failure modes and evidence: `references/methods.md`. Section numbers
like (3.2) refer to that file.

## 3. Keep / donate / sell / discard criteria (apply in this order)

1. Hazard, broken, expired, recalled, or contaminated -> discard or hazardous-waste.
2. Not yours -> belongs to someone else: ask them, never decide for them.
3. Required by law/finance -> keep (tax, IDs, warranties); scan or file; see paper rule.
4. Used in last 12 months, or seasonal/annual use with a home that fits -> keep.
5. Loved or meaningful AND fits its container -> keep; sentimental has its own cap.
6. Duplicate beyond the container cap -> release the weakest.
7. Aspirational/"someday" (hobby, size, project) -> set a dated test (90 days); else go.
8. Replaceable cheaply and quickly -> release (with the 20/20 caveat above).
9. Sell vs donate: sell only if (value x probability x effort) beats your time; as a
   guide, items worth under about $30 are donated; bulky items worth more go to a single
   listing batch with a deadline; unsold after two weeks -> donate [professional practice,
   rule of thumb, not sourced]. Set a hard date by which the box leaves the house.
10. Gifts with guilt, inherited items: photograph, keep one representative piece, offer
    relatives first. Heirloom decision is the owner's, never the helper's.
Paper rule [professional practice]: shred/recycle anything findable online; keep legal,
tax, medical, identity, warranty; scan the rest into folders with a naming rule.
Decision aids that the research does support: the endowment effect inflates the value of
owned things, so ask "if I did not own this, would I buy it today at this price, and where
would it live?" (see the research table in references). Sunk cost: judge on future use, not what was paid.

## 4. Session loop (what a good session looks like)

1. Define the target: one zone, one outcome, one stop time.
2. Empty and group: take everything out of the zone; gather like with like.
3. Decide per item with the criteria in 4; trash first, keep set last.
4. Assign homes by frequency of use and by where you would look first.
5. Bag outbound; remove from the house today; schedule the donation/sale trip.
6. Contain only what remains; label if the household needs it.
7. Reset the room to usable before stopping; take an after photo; log time and counts.
Facilitation rules [professional practice]:
- Client decides; the helper prompts with questions, not verdicts.
- Use "unsure" bins sparingly; time-box sentimental sessions.
- Offer a break when decisions slow down; slow decisions are a signal, not laziness.
- A family session: agree on rules for shared spaces; each person owns their own pile.
- Keep momentum: start with easy wins; end a session on a finished zone.

## 5. Maintenance and relapse prevention

- Containers as caps (White): when full, nothing new without something out.
- Weekly 15-minute reset, same time, same place, tied to an existing cue
  (implementation intention: "after the weekend family dinner, I clear the entry table").
- Entry-point control: shoes/bags/mail/school paper each get a landing spot; process mail daily.
- Quarterly mini-edit per category (clothes by season, toys at birthdays and holidays).
- One-in-one-out for clothes, toys, books; a standing donation bag that leaves monthly.
- Teach the "why": the Minimalists note decluttering without purpose refills; Dana White
  says to adjust containers, not abandon them.
- Measure: photos, number of bags out, minutes to find a standard item, a 1-5 stress or
  satisfaction score before/after, the Clutter Quality of Life Scale if available.
- Expect relapse; a single missed day does not break a habit [research], repeated lapses
  do. Re-start with the smallest zone.
- BCPO follow-up domain asks the professional to evaluate whether changes are effective,
  sustainable, and whether skills transferred to the client [professional practice].

## 6. Honest limits (read before speaking)

- No trial shows any named method beats another. Method-matching below is professional logic.
- Do not tell the family clutter "raises cortisol" as fact; say a small study linked a
  stressful-home impression to flatter cortisol patterns in women.
- Do not promise "66 days" or "21 days"; say about two to three months of consistency on
  average, with wide variation, and about half of the study's sample did not fit the model.
- Decision-fatigue advice is a pragmatic precaution, not a proven mechanism.
- The profession: NAPO's ethics code asks for competence, honest representation of
  qualifications, referral when unqualified, confidentiality, advance fee disclosure, and
  recommendations in the client's interest. The CPO credential requires 1,500 paid hours
  in five years, an exam with six domains (preliminary communication 12%, assessment 18%,
  project plan 16%, implementation 31%, follow-up 12%, legal/ethical 11%), and the BCPO
  code. Behave accordingly: scope-limited, honest, referral-ready.

## 7. Quick scripts for the agent

- Opening: "What would a good day in this room look like, and what stops that today?"
- Stuck on an item: "If you did not own it, would you buy it today? Where would it live?"
- Guilt gift: "Your gratitude was the gift. The object can leave."
- Disagreement: separate piles by owner; shared items decided by the person who uses them most; escalate to the household vision (3.4).
- Kids: offer 2-3 choices, not open lists; use containers; praise the finished zone.
- Overwhelm: "Pick the smallest visible spot, set a 15-minute timer, bag only trash first."

## Pitfalls
- Buying containers before the keep set is known.
- A "maybe" or "relocate" pile that becomes permanent; outbound bags that stay in the house.
- Using joy or 20/20 tests on heirlooms, tools used yearly, or hard-to-replace items.
- Continuing the method menu when safety flags or acute distress appear (switch to harm reduction).
- Deciding about another adult's things.

## Verification
- The chosen method and why were stated plainly to the household.
- Each session ended with outbound items out of the house, an after photo, the next target, and one
  maintenance rule.
- Claims to the family match "Honest limits".
