# Worldbuilding exposition without info-dumps

## Incluing (Jo Walton)
Walton coined "incluing": "the process of giving information subtly without resorting to an infodump"
(jowaltonbooks.com, "4th April 2003: Mode"). Elsewhere she describes it as scattering pieces of information
seamlessly through the text so they add up to a big picture. The reader builds the world from the clues
(quoted in Futurismic, 2010). Wikipedia ("Exposition (narrative)") gives the common short form: "scattering
information seamlessly through the text, as opposed to stopping the story to impart the information."

## Turkey City Lexicon (SFWA; Shiner and Sterling), Part Five "Background"
- **Infodump:** a large chunk of indigestible expository matter. Brief, deft infodumps are fine
  ("kuttnering"). Information worked into the story's structure is "heinleining".
- **The Edges of Ideas:** skip the mechanism and show the impact on people's lives.
- **Frontloading:** too much exposition at the start (Connie Willis). **Nowhere Nowhen:** too little.
- **"I've suffered for my Art":** research inflicted on the reader. "Homework exists to make the difficult
  look easy" (Budrys).
- **Call a Rabbit a Smeerp:** renaming ordinary things for false exoticism (Blish).
- **Stapledon:** a lecturing voice that takes centre stage.
- Part Six: **Viewpoint glitch:** relating something the POV character could not know.

## Techniques
1. **Carry it on a prop or readout:** an in-world display, label or document teaches the world in the
   character's own medium.
2. **Carry it on an argument:** economics, risk or rules are taught through a disagreement about what to do.
3. **Contradiction as wonder:** let the POV register that something does not fit and move on, conveying
   strangeness without explaining it.
4. **One clause of recap at most** when a chapter opens on the previous one's material.
5. **Native terms:** gloss once at first occurrence (or per house rule); render native units literally.
6. **Real-world leakage:** search for real place names, deities, units and brands. Each is either
   deliberate (in canon) or a break.
