# Planetary science and astrobiology

Source labels resolve in `sources.md`.

## Build a coupled world

Check these together rather than one at a time:

1. **Star:** luminosity, spectrum, age, flares, UV/X-ray environment.
2. **Orbit:** semi-major axis, eccentricity, period, obliquity, rotation, tides.
3. **Planet:** radius, mass, density, surface gravity, escape velocity, internal heat.
4. **Atmosphere:** pressure, composition, greenhouse effect, clouds, circulation, escape.
5. **Surface/interior:** oceans, continents, volcanism, tectonics, carbon cycling, albedo.
6. **Life:** solvent, energy gradients, nutrients, metabolism, ecology, evolutionary time.

NASA's planetary fact sheets show that gravity, escape velocity, temperature, orbit, and atmosphere vary independently enough that “Earth-size” does not mean “Earth-like” [7]. Axial tilt and eccentricity affect season strength and length [6].

## Habitability tests

- A habitable zone means surface liquid water may be possible; it is a first cut, not proof. Atmosphere, size, and stellar stability remain necessary context [8].
- Ask whether the atmosphere survives the star's radiation and the planet's gravity over geological time.
- Check water phase against pressure as well as temperature.
- A lush biosphere requires energy and material cycles, not only water and oxygen.
- An ecosystem needs producers/energy harvesters, consumers, decomposers or recycling, and constraints on population growth.
- Alien biochemistry may differ, but the text should signal departures from life-as-we-know-it rather than accidentally mixing incompatible assumptions.

## Biosignatures

- Treat a detected gas or surface feature as a candidate biosignature. NASA notes that even oxygen may be generated abiotically and should be interpreted with companion gases and environmental context [10].
- Require multiple converging lines of evidence where characters make a strong life claim: atmosphere + surface + time variability + chemistry, for example [8][9][10].
- Separate “habitable,” “inhabited,” “intelligent,” and “technological.” They are not synonyms.
- The National Academies frames astrobiology around life's origin, evolution, distribution, future, and the technology needed to search; a credible review should preserve uncertainty [9].

## Climate and ecology red flags

- Seasons attributed only to changing star distance on a low-eccentricity Earth analogue.
- Identical climate at all latitudes despite ordinary rotation, tilt, and circulation.
- Global weather change without an energy or circulation mechanism.
- Complete ecological silence on a thriving world, unless localized or explicitly uncanny.
- Predators with no food base; large animals with no plausible energy budget.
- Instant adaptation by evolution. Individuals acclimate; populations evolve across generations.

## Exception boundary

A science-fantasy world may contain an engineered or magical planet-scale system. Label the controlled
feature and keep adjacent ordinary consequences coherent: "the clouds move deliberately" can be an
exception, but ordinary air still has pressure, weather, drag, heat transport and ecological effects
unless canon says otherwise.

Minimal repair pattern:
- Keep the image.
- Name one controlling exception ("the weather engine pulled the cloud bands").
- Restore one ordinary consequence ("the pressure front still hit minutes later").
- Do not explain withheld lore.
