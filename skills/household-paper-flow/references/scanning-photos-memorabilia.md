# Scanning, photos and memorabilia

## 6. Scanning and digital organization

**When to scan:** warranties, receipts you might need, manuals, insurance, medical, kid artwork and school records, and any vital document. Everything else: shred or recycle. If the paper does not need to be kept legally (see the retention table in SKILL.md and `retention-details.md`) and you have a scan, shred. [FTC]

**Setup (one hour):** flatbed or sheet-fed scanner or the phone app that makes searchable PDFs; 200-300 dpi grayscale for documents is enough (judgment; LOC says 300-400 dpi for snapshot prints). A sheet-fed document scanner is the biggest time saver.

**Folder structure mirrors the paper cabinet.** **File naming:** `YYYY-MM-DD_Source_What.pdf` (LOC suggests starting names with year and month so files sort in time order). [LOC]

**Backups:** 3-2-1 (judgment): three copies, two kinds of media, one off-site. LOC: store copies in separate places and migrate about every five years. [LOC]

**Security:** encrypt the archive; password manager for logins; do not email scans of SSN cards; two-factor on the cloud account; give a trusted person emergency access (see `emergency-binder.md`). [ready.gov: password-protected, encrypted format]

**Workflow:** scan weekly in the same session as paper processing, 10-15 minutes. Backlog: do 30 minutes per day, newest first, and stop at 3-4 hours per sitting.

**Time (judgment):** a sheet-fed scanner does about 20-30 pages per minute on a good day; plan 1 hour per 100 pages, including prep.


## 8. Photos, memorabilia and kids' papers

### Photos and slides [LOC; NARA]
1. **Sort first, scan second.** Cull duplicates and blurry; keep the best 20-30 percent (judgment).
2. **Clean:** lint-free cloth dampened with water on the scanner glass; anti-static cloth on the photo; handle by edges. [LOC]
3. **Resolution [LOC]:** 300-400 dpi for typical snapshot prints; slides and small prints around 1,400-1,500 dpi; negatives 1,500-2,000 dpi. Use 24-bit color for color, 8-bit grayscale for black and white.
4. **Format:** save a TIFF master (LZW compression is lossless), and make JPEG copies for sharing. [LOC]
5. **Name and back up** as in the scanning section above. Add year and who/where in the filename; write names on the back of originals with a soft pencil (judgment).
6. **Outsourcing:** service bureaus charge per item; a bulk service can be cheaper than months of time (price not verified). Use one that returns originals and ask where they ship them.

### Storing originals [NARA]
- Cool (below 75 F), relative humidity below 65 percent (but above 15 percent), and not in damp basements, garages or hot attics; away from pipes, windows and leaks; on a shelf off the floor.
- Use acid-free, lignin-free boxes and folders and enclosures that passed the Photographic Activity Test; do not overstuff; polyester L-sleeves for brittle sheets.
- Avoid rubber cement, tape, non-stainless clips and rubber bands, magnetic albums, unknown plastics and PVC.
- Color negatives, slides and prints fade within decades at room temperature (digitize them first).

### Kids' memorabilia (judgment)
- One keepsake box per child (plastic lidded tub with archival folders), sized for a shoebox-plus; a "box full" rule means curate, not buy a bigger box.
- Photograph large artwork and 3D projects; keep one or two physical samples per year; put the rest in the donate/trash bin after the child chooses.
- Keep school records, report cards and awards in a binder; scan; keep vital items (immunization record, birth certificate) separately locked.

### Sentimental paper
Do last, in short sessions. Ask relatives whether they want family papers before discarding (AARP).
