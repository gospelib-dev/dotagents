# Plausible-versus-magic boundary

Source labels resolve in `sources.md`.

## Genre rule

Hard SF uses established or carefully extrapolated science as a backbone and should not willfully break known principles, while science fantasy commonly blends SF and fantasy and may use other dimensions, psi, or quasirationalized magic [16][17]. A science-fantasy book is not a hard-SF proof exercise; a hard-SF book is held closer to the line.

The review target is therefore **legible contract**, not universal realism:
- ordinary events obey ordinary science;
- future technology pays visible costs and keeps consistent limits;
- canon-designated exceptional effects (magic, precursor technology, the numinous) may violate physics where canon permits;
- the exception does not spread to unrelated systems by convenience.

## Boundary test

For each impossible effect answer:
1. **Marker:** What tells a reader this is exceptional—source, visual language, prior setup, or character reaction?
2. **Scope:** Exactly which object/process is exempt?
3. **Cost:** What power, pain, risk, sacrifice, delay, uncertainty, or access limit remains?
4. **Consistency:** Has the same effect obeyed the same rule before?
5. **Knowledge:** Could the POV character name the mechanism, or should the prose stay sensory?
6. **Plot use:** Does the effect solve a conflict? If so, was that capability understood beforehand?

Sanderson's First Law is a useful craft diagnostic: conflict-solving power should scale with reader understanding [18]. His Second Law prioritizes limitations over powers because constraints create struggle and consequential choices [19]. These are craft guidelines, not scientific laws.

## Four verdicts

- **Pass—ordinary:** plausible enough at the story's precision; no fix.
- **Pass—exception:** impossible but clearly a canon-designated exception and canon-consistent; preserve mystery.
- **Boundary leak:** effect may be allowed, but ordinary-adjacent claims use false mechanism or overreach. Change the local wording, not the exception.
- **Rule break:** contradicts established capability, limit, or cost. Report as science/technology continuity and cite canon.

## Examples

- Structure-borne deck vibration in vacuum: ordinary pass. Audible boom through empty space: ordinary failure unless received by radio/structure.
- A ghost stepping out of an enchanted mirror: exception pass if canon-consistent. A nearby unpowered steel door melting without being touched by that field: separate claim needing a marker.
- Oxygen-rich air supporting fierce combustion: ordinary. “The atmosphere ignited” suggests the air itself is fuel; prefer weapon-driven fire/thermal runaway unless an exotic reactant is established [20].
- Direct mind-to-mind memory sharing: exception pass. Claiming an ordinary radio carried a telepathic message would break canon if canon says telepathy bypasses devices.

## Gated-lore restraint

A scientific fix must not explain withheld lore (the project's gated mysteries) beyond the POV's allowed knowledge. Prefer sensory evidence and observed limits. If the smallest accurate fix would expose gated lore, report the issue and ask for an owner/canon decision rather than inventing exposition.
