# Foreshadowing and payoff

## Chekhov
"One must not put a loaded rifle on the stage if no one is thinking of firing it." Letter to Aleksandr
Semenovich Lazarev (A. S. Gruzinsky), 1 November 1889, as cited by the Isaiah Berlin Virtual Library
(Wikipedia "Chekhov's gun"). A popular variant ends "It's wrong to make promises you don't mean to keep."
Swain makes the same point as reader psychology: the reader assumes every spotlighted stimulus will pay off,
and an unused one is a "false plant" that earns resentment (Techniques of the Selling Writer, ch. 3).

## Promise, progress, payoff (Brandon Sanderson, BYU lectures 2020 and 2025, Lecture 2 "Plot")
- Promises come in three kinds: tone, character arc and plot. Every new character and every chapter
  carries small promises.
- **Progress** is what keeps pages turning. "A big reason that people drop off a book is that there weren't
  enough signposts of progress."
- **Payoff** is "a surprising but fulfilling answer to your promise": surprising yet inevitable. You make
  it surprising with plausible deniability (obstacles, escalations, red herrings).
- A twist is promising one thing and then making readers wish you had promised another.
- In-scene action sequences should be small plots with their own promise, progress and payoff.

## Sanderson's First Law (essay, Feb 2007)
"An author's ability to solve conflict with magic is DIRECTLY PROPORTIONAL to how well the reader
understands said magic." With soft magic, don't let the magic solve problems. Use it for wonder, or to make
things worse. Soft systems may cost, complicate or reveal until their rules are on the page; only then may
they resolve a conflict. No unearned rescue.

## Plant hygiene
- Seeds work best in-scene, sensory and POV-inferred, and short (a sentence or two). Teach a meaning once.
- Withheld lore is felt, never explained.
- Early orphan seeds may legitimately stay as texture; do not "fix" them by bolting on payoffs if the
  project has decided otherwise.

## Tests
1. **Ledger:** for each spotlighted item, record plant line → payoff line, or "texture".
2. **Warning stack:** count the ominous clauses before a planted failure. At 3 or more, the failure feels
   announced and the protagonist looks careless. Keep one defect and one mitigation.
3. **Backward grep:** every payoff image ("the letter she never sent") must have its plant on the page
   earlier.
4. **Invisible action:** a consequence the reader must register later (an absence, a loss) needs one
   observable on-page beat.
