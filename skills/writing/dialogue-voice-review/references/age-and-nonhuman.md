# Age-appropriate and non-human voice

## Teen voice: developmental range, not a costume

Authentic YA voice cannot be reduced to current slang; teen readers notice adult ventriloquism [S3]. A useful practitioner rule is “true, not necessarily transcript-real”: preserve age, background, place, and readable compression [S4].


### Review dimensions

- **Autonomy:** who controls the choice, resource, body, or information?
- **Peer/family position:** teasing, face-saving, belonging, challenge, and repair.
- **Pragmatics:** how meaning changes with audience and context; adolescent pragmatic competence is still developing, and indirect affective replies recruit social-perspective systems strongly [S6].
- **Reasoning:** do not equate youth with simple thought. Older adolescents in one study used more evaluating/creating responses, while syntactic complexity and semantic diversity did not simply separate age groups [S5].
- **Conversation:** peer talk may use varied question types, figurative language, abrupt topic shifts, entertaining, and persuasion [S7]. Marker *choice*, not raw marker frequency, distinguishes bonding from informative teen talk [S9].
- **Durability:** prefer relationship-specific teasing and invented/world slang over trend-chasing [S4].

### Teen failure checks

- Slang can be swapped among every teen without changing identity.
- A fifteen-year-old speaks only in polished thematic summaries.
- Competence erases humor, insecurity, peer awareness, or family history.
- The adult narrator diagnoses feelings the teen has not framed that way.
- Every teen is fragmentary, impulsive, or anti-intellectual.

## Child voice: concrete does not mean shallow

ASHA’s benchmarks show school-age growth in topic management, persuasion, inference, figurative language, and listener adaptation while warning that culture, language, and neurodiversity affect timing [S8]. Use them as ranges, never ceilings.

### Separate four layers

1. **Vocabulary:** concrete/familiar vs abstract/specialist.
2. **Syntax:** clause load, repair, repetition, question chains.
3. **Cognition:** what the child can infer or plan.
4. **Burden:** adult-like vigilance can be learned behavior without adult diction.

Example: protect a responsible child's short assertions and caretaking behavior, or a curious child's concrete observation and question chains. Neither should sound generically “cute.”


## Non-human voice: familiar bridge plus alien constraint

Writing Excuses recommends a reader bridge through recognizable needs while retaining the non-human feature that justifies the character [S12]. Linguistic analysis of a non-human character shows that both a stable repertoire and meaningful style shifts can construct identity and persona [S11].

### Eight constraints to test

| Constraint | Review question |
|---|---|
| Sensorium | What data arrives first: light, pressure, chemistry, network, markings? |
| Body | What can this body point, hide, touch, hold, or fail to do? |
| Cognition | Does it classify, distribute, remember, or decide differently? |
| Time | Does urgency, patience, age, or memory operate on another scale? |
| Social role | Is speech personal, collective, ritual, ranked, or translated? |
| Knowledge | What concepts/names are unavailable or untranslatable? |
| Medium | Words, color, gesture, glyphs, optics, latency, static, silence? |
| Desire | What recognizable need lets the reader enter the scene? |

### Application pattern

- **Robot/AI companion:** a recognizable need (family, loyalty) is the bridge; diagnostics, literal categories, memory gaps, optics/displays, mechanical embodiment and processing latency keep it non-human.
- **Translated alien speakers:** translated English never carries the whole utterance; gesture, colour or other signals are part of voice.
- **Ancient or vast beings:** scale, collective/singular authority and cadence matter more than faux-archaic vocabulary.
- **Ceremonial roles:** formality does not grant decision authority the role lacks.
- **Alien bodies:** let the body make emotion legible (unusual limbs, eyes, colour) while goals and patter supply the familiar bridge.

### Failure checks

- Species voice = one accent or tic.
- All members of a species speak identically.
- Robot voice = emotionless grammar.
- Alien voice = archaic or cryptic diction without cultural cause.
- Translation erases gesture, color, medium, or body.
- Non-human character suddenly knows a human/reader concept the scene has not taught.
