# Fiction and YA presentation reference

Apply only after the form is linguistically correct.

## What a language switch must accomplish

Literary code-switching can signal precision, connotation, social identity, realism, irony, mood, intimacy, or play [S15]. In each scene, name the switch's function. If removing the conlang changes nothing in voice, world, relationship, ritual, or plot, treat it as a readability suggestion—not a linguistic error.


Albakry and Hancock show that literary switching can convey social/cultural norms and mood, and that its density may track narrative necessity [S16]. Do not impose a fixed words-per-page quota. Judge each switch by function and reader access.

## Reader-access ladder

Prefer the least intrusive method that preserves the scene:

1. **Action/reaction:** the response reveals enough meaning.
2. **Apposition:** `term, brief English sense`.
3. **Integrated translation:** the POV or an in-world translator device supplies the meaning without stopping the scene.
4. **Echo/repetition:** another speaker restates it naturally.
5. **Delayed inference:** only when mystery/gating is intentional and the immediate plot remains legible.
6. **Glossary:** supplementary, never the sole support for a plot-critical line.

Pires's study of created-language dialogue finds that glosses and contextual development can let readers deduce referents while preserving fictional authenticity [S14].

## First-use test

For each unfamiliar form ask:

- Is this the first reader encounter or merely the first encounter for this POV?
- Is its meaning required now?
- Does surrounding action constrain the meaning enough?
- Was the meaning already taught? Many house styles permit teaching a meaning only once.
- Would an explicit gloss violate withheld lore?
- Does the English gloss match the literal morphology and the speech act?

A first plot-critical term needs one clear access path. A later repetition normally needs none. Never stack italics, apposition, parenthetical definition, and narrator explanation for the same meaning.

## Typography and naming

MLA's general rule italicizes foreign terms in English but exempts proper nouns and whole foreign-language quotations [S13]. The project's house style controls the manuscript, typically:


- proper names remain roman;
- conlang words and phrases follow the manuscript's established italics pattern;
- capitalization follows grammar and sentence position;
- apostrophes and diacritics are semantic/phonological data, not decoration;
- repeated styling must be consistent across the error class.

Do not “correct” house style to MLA when the project deliberately differs.

## Translation devices

An in-world translator device or the narrator may translate speech, but the output must still preserve:


- who is agent, patient, possessor, and addressee;
- number distinctions, especially dual and collective;
- evidential force when scene-relevant;
- politeness, honorifics, taboo, and ritual register;
- ambiguity that the source actually has;
- no meaning that the morphology cannot carry.

When the story prints both original and translation, audit them as a pair. A fluent English gloss cannot excuse malformed conlang.

## Audience and genre cautions

- **Younger readers:** prioritize immediate scene comprehension, not linguistic lecture. Use concrete action and short integrated cues.
- **Withheld lore:** opacity can be required. Confirm the reader can follow the scene even if the cosmology remains uninterpreted.
- **Machine translation in-world:** separate in-world device output from editorial glossing; mistranslation must be intentional and canon-supported.
- **Ritual language:** longer formulae earn space when the ritual and speaker identity matter, but still require a valid parse.
- **Names:** test phonotactics and attestation even when a reader does not need a gloss.

## Finding threshold

**Required:** wrong form, parse, meaning, register, knowledge gate, or a plot-critical utterance whose intended action cannot be understood.

**Suggestion:** canon-correct language whose presentation could be lighter or clearer without changing meaning.

Always preserve classes: one finding lists every instance of the same token or failed rule.
