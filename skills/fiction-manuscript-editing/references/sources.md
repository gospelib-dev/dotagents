# Sources

Accessed 2026-11-14 unless a publication is cited. Project-internal sources belong in the project's own skill.

## Professional standards and editorial scope

**[S1] Editors' Association of Canada. _Professional Editorial Standards 2024_.** Defines structural, stylistic, copy, and proof stages; scope, audience, voice preservation, style sheets, actionable queries, fiction exceptions, and narrative rhythm. https://editors.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/EditorsCanada_ProfessionalEditorialStandards_2024.pdf

**[S2] Editorial Freelancers Association. “Editorial Service Definitions.”** Defines developmental, line, and copyediting functions and deliverables. https://the-efa.org/editorial-services-definitions

**[S3] Chartered Institute of Editing and Proofreading. “The Role and Purpose of the Editorial Profession.”** Centers accuracy, clarity, readability, intended audience, author voice, and avoidance of over-intervention. https://ciep.uk/resource/the-role-and-purpose-of-the-editorial-profession.html

**[S4] Chartered Institute of Editing and Proofreading. “What Are Queries, and How Should an Author Respond?”** Explains when editors query ambiguity, contradiction, missing information, and changes requiring approval. https://ciep.uk/resource/queries-author.html

**[S5] Chartered Institute of Editing and Proofreading. “Creating Style Sheets for Fiction.”** Covers SPaG choices plus POV, tense, characters, timeline, plot, and plot threads. https://ciep.uk/resource/fiction-style-sheets.html

## Fiction copy and editorial relationships

**[S6] _The Chicago Manual of Style Online_, 18th ed. “Quotations and Dialogue” Q&A.** Dialogue/action-beat punctuation, em dashes for interruption, and three-point ellipses for fictional trailing/faltering speech. https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/qanda/data/faq/topics/Quotations.html?page=2 and https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/qanda/data/faq/topics/Quotations/faq0047.html

**[S7] Amy J. Schneider. _The Chicago Guide to Copyediting Fiction_. University of Chicago Press, 2023.** Fiction-specific workflow, style sheets, characters, places, timeline, grammar, dialogue, and fact/fiction issues. https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/C/bo183690033.html

**[S8] Carol Fisher Saller. _The Subversive Copy Editor_, 2nd ed. University of Chicago Press, 2016.** Advocates carefulness, transparency, flexibility, and working with the writer for the reader. https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/S/bo23530364.html

## YA audience and critique

**[S9] Young Adult Library Services Association, American Library Association. “About the Michael L. Printz Award.”** Defines YA eligibility as ages 12–18. https://www.ala.org/yalsa/booklistsawards/bookawards/printzaward/aboutprintz/aboutaward

**[S10] Society of Children's Book Writers and Illustrators, California Central/Coastal. “SCBWI Critique Gold Form with Talking Points.”** Tests plot/character arc, pacing, POV, dialogue, audience level, teen concern, and protagonist agency. https://www.scbwi.org/regions/cencal/scbwi-critique-gold-form-with-talking-points

**[S11] Society of Children's Book Writers and Illustrators, California Central/Coastal. “Checklist: Preparing Your Manuscript for Critique.”** Tests audience fit, viewpoint, character change, voice, conflict, setting, and overused language. https://www.scbwi.org/regions/cencal/checklist-preparing-your-manuscript-for-critique

## Structure and actionable feedback

**[S12] Sangeeta Mehta. “Before You Hire a Developmental Editor: What You Need to Know.” _Jane Friedman_, 2022; Q&A with Julie Scheina and Susan Chang.** Distinguishes developmental feedback and emphasizes specific, actionable written notes. https://janefriedman.com/before-you-hire-a-developmental-editor-what-you-need-to-know

**[S13] Robert McKee. “Do Your Scenes Turn?” McKee Story.** Tests whether conflict produces a perceptible value change and challenges exposition-only scenes. https://mckeestory.com/do-your-scenes-turn

**[S14] Allegra Huston. “The Two Basic Rules of Editing (and the Rookie Mistake).” _Jane Friedman_, 2019; excerpt from _How to Work with a Writer_.** Recommends identifying strengths and using questions rather than imposing a single fix. https://janefriedman.com/the-two-basic-rules-of-editing-and-the-rookie-mistake

**[S15] Sarah Chauncey. “Manuscript Evaluations: What They Are and What to Expect.” _Jane Friedman_, 2019.** Separates high-level evaluation from developmental editing and explains how inline examples make abstract notes concrete. https://janefriedman.com/manuscript-evaluations-what-they-are-and-what-to-expect/

**[S16] Christine Steele and Lourdes Venard. _The Collaborative Editor: How to Build Trust with Writers Through Queries and Other Communications_. University of Chicago Press, 2026.** Covers collaboration, feedback, querying, tone, reports, and contextual sensitivity. https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/C/bo288180043.html
