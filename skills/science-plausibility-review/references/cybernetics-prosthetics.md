# Cybernetics and prosthetics

Source labels resolve in `sources.md`.

## Five layers to identify

1. **Body state:** retained limb or amputation; atrophy, scar, bone, nerves, blood supply, skin.
2. **Attachment:** socket/brace, straps, osseointegration, surgical implant, external frame.
3. **Actuation:** passive spring, motor, powered joint, exoskeletal assistance.
4. **Control:** body motion, myoelectric signal, implanted electrode, peripheral nerve, brain interface.
5. **Feedback:** vision, sound, vibration, pressure, touch, temperature, pain, proprioception.

Do not collapse these layers into “cybernetic.” Each can fail separately.

## Real-world grounding

- Sensory feedback is important but interacts with vision, training, and motor control; laboratory gains do not automatically become real-life performance [11].
- Osseointegration can improve attachment, range of motion, signal stability, and embodiment, while preserving risks such as infection and implant failure [13].
- An osseointegrated neural interface that houses rerouted nerves/electrodes is a proposed design; the cited human work is anatomical/cadaver feasibility, not proof of routine clinical performance [12].
- Neural control needs calibration, training, latency handling, power, and failure states. Sensory return should identify which modality and body map receives it [11][12][13].

## Review checklist

- Is the device a brace around a retained limb or a replacement limb? Use the canon term consistently.
- Where does load enter the body? Check pressure points, skin breakdown, bone stress, and alignment.
- How much torque/power does the joint need, and where does waste heat go?
- What happens when power falls: locked, limp, passive support, safe mode, or collapse?
- Can the user feel position without looking? If yes, state the proprioceptive/nerve pathway or canon exception.
- Does pain indicate tissue, nerve, fit, overheating, or an interface signal? Keep those distinct.
- After repair or transformation, preserve rehabilitation consequences unless the same explicit exception restores tissue and neural mapping.
- Search all chapters for the same device failure class; temperature, power, signal, and mechanical faults should recur consistently.

## Application patterns

- A brace around a retained limb is not a replacement limb; use the canon term consistently.
- A sensory prosthesis (e.g. a visual aid) has defined sensing limits; it is not an all-purpose narrator or
  a carrier for channels canon assigns elsewhere.
- Network or magical responses of a device may be canon exceptions; ordinary fit, fatigue, power depletion,
  tissue injury and rehabilitation remain reviewable.
- An artificial body created or altered by an exceptional process is an explicit exception: preserve established form and
  limits; do not demand biological anatomy unless the prose asserts it literally.

## Minimal fixes

- Replace “prosthetic leg” with “powered brace” where the retained limb matters.
- Name the missing channel: “the brace returned joint angle, not touch.”
- Add one line of calibration, charging, chafing, safe-mode behavior, or recovery.
- Change literal tissue damage (“muscles tearing”) to severe exertion (“muscles straining”) unless later consequences are intended.
