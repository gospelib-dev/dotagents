# Dialogue and relationship diagnostics

## Seven-axis fingerprint

| Axis | Questions | Useful evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Lexicon | What nouns/verbs does this person reach for? | Work, family, body, ritual, trade, mechanism |
| Syntax | Short/long, paratactic/subordinate, fragments/periodic? | Sentence lengths and clause shape [S1][S10] |
| Grammar | Contractions, modality, politeness, certainty? | `might` vs `will`; formal vs casual forms |
| Turn design | Questions, interruptions, overlaps, repair, silence? | Who holds/yields/redirects the floor [S7][S9] |
| Address | Names, titles, kin terms, avoidance? | Relationship and knowledge state |
| Figurative system | What can this person compare? | Sensorium, culture, profession, memory |
| Embodiment | What happens around speech? | Human gesture or species-specific signal |

A character need not display all seven in every scene. Repetition of one conspicuous tic is weaker than a stable combination. Computational work finds fictional speakers distinguishable through lexical and syntactic patterns and treats the result as probabilistic, not an absolute test [S10].

## Tagless cold-read procedure

1. Sample five lines across low pressure, conflict, intimacy, exposition, and decision where available.
2. Strip tags, names, and unique lore nouns that give the answer away.
3. Attribute each line and name the axis that supports the guess.
4. Treat fewer than 4/5 as a trigger. Re-read the scene before flagging; disguise, translation, and stress can intentionally narrow differences.
5. If a defect remains, identify the missing axis and every other line in that class.

## Verbal-action and subtext pass

McKee defines dialogue as action taken to satisfy a need: identify want, intent, and tactic beneath the line [S2]. Use this ledger:

| Line | Surface | Private pressure | Tactic | Uptake | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| “Fine.” | Agreement | Avoid conflict | Close topic | Listener distrusts | Task proceeds badly |

A line can be direct and still work when directness **is** the risky action: confession, command in danger, child request, testimony, ritual, or a relationship milestone. The defect is not literalness; it is unearned or consequence-free literalness.

## Relationship-earned test

A relationship beat is earned when the page supplies most of this chain:

1. **Prior pattern:** what each person normally withholds or does.
2. **Pressure:** why the normal pattern cannot continue.
3. **Risk:** what the speaker may lose by changing register.
4. **Reciprocity:** listener answers in words, behavior, or meaningful silence.
5. **Consequence:** the exchange changes a choice, boundary, task, or future address.
6. **Carry-forward:** later behavior does not instantly reset without cause.

Do not require indirectness everywhere. Adolescent readers process context-dependent indirect affective meaning, but direct speech remains natural where social action requires it [S6].

## Tags and beats

- Attribution is successful when the reader knows who speaks without stopping.
- `said` and `asked` are useful defaults; marked tags should add information the line and context do not already carry [S13].
- A smile, laugh, sigh, look, or motion is a separate action, not automatically a speech verb [S13].
- Beats earn space by orienting bodies, revealing character, carrying subtext, escalating pressure, or changing blocking [S14].
- Replace generic beats with motive-linked tasks: repair, inventory, watch a doorway, hide an object, check a child, dim optics.
- Use new paragraphs for speaker changes and standard punctuation for tags [S15].

## Revision patterns

### Homogenized line

Before: two siblings explain danger in the same complete, technical sentence.

Fix: keep facts stable; let the analytical sibling name mechanism/next action while the relational sibling names who gets hurt and challenges the choice.

### On-the-nose line

Before: “I am afraid you will leave me.”

Possible fix: “You packed already?” plus blocking toward the door. Keep the direct version if confession is the earned turn and its risk changes the scene.

### Random beat

Before: She looked away, shrugged, and touched her hair.

Fix: use one action tied to the live task or cut all three.

### Non-human flattening

Before: the robot’s only marker is avoiding contractions.

Fix: preserve diagnostics, latency, sensor focus, body architecture, literal classification, and relational purpose; allow warmth to act through those channels.
