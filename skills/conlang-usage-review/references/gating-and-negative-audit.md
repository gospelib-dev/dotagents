# Conlang and gated-term review

Use this depth pass whenever a chapter contains constructed-language words, culturally restricted names, translated phrases, or proper terms with a staged reveal.

## Extract and classify

Collect every occurrence, not only unique forms. Record the chapter line, speaker or POV holder, formatting, immediate meaning, and all synchronized occurrences in the chapter outline, info, summary, and object/character tracking docs.

Classify each token as:

- native vocabulary or inflection;
- loanword or sacred/taboo form;
- personal/place name;
- people/species term;
- technology/object term;
- untranslated cultural concept;
- gated proper term.

A catalog miss is `unknown` until context proves an error. It is not automatically a misspelling.

## Validate in this order

1. Exact spelling, apostrophe form, diacritics, capitalization, and italics.
2. Lexical meaning and part of speech in the vocabulary source.
3. Grammar: noun class, number, case, agreement, derivation, and word order.
4. Phonology and explicit loanword/exception rules.
5. Contextual meaning: verify that a people-name is not being used where the language has a distinct technology term, or vice versa.
6. Character knowledge: verify the speaker or POV holder has crossed the reveal epoch, not merely that the term appeared somewhere earlier in the book.
7. Glossing: confirm the reader has enough local or previously established meaning without repeating a gloss mechanically.

When two canon files repeat a form but the authoritative language lexicon distinguishes the meanings, report the semantic conflict and list every propagation site. Do not let repetition turn an error into evidence of correctness.

## Audit non-lexical language systems

Treat culturally encoded body displays, color meanings, title/name registers, ritual typography, and glossing conventions as conlang usage rather than leaving them to a generic continuity pass.

- Inventory every in-scope body display or marking occurrence, not only explicit conlang tokens. Separate environmental light/color from culturally meaningful body language.
- Check each display against both the meaning ledger and the prose-rendering rule. A color can be semantically correct but still violate style canon when narration repeats an already-taught gloss instead of showing color plus behavior.
- Check formal and casual name forms against scene register, especially council proceedings, ceremonies, introductions, and intimate dialogue.
- When only one occurrence fails, cite the full inventory or the passing classes that prove it is isolated; do not claim `only one` after checking only the suspicious line.

## Gating decisions

Check the term's first allowed epoch, who teaches or reveals it, and what the current character actually knows. Distinguish:

- a forbidden proper name from an ordinary lowercase adjective;
- the character's mistaken in-scene impression from an omniscient doc statement;
- a hostile or untranslated first hearing from the later meaning reveal;
- narrator vocabulary from dialogue vocabulary.

A term can be correctly spelled and still be premature. Conversely, a pre-reveal term is allowed when canon explicitly assigns it to the character's current cultural vocabulary.

## Negative audit when no conlang appears

Do not stop at “no conlang tokens.” Prove the negative result with three inventories:

1. Search the chapter for every gated proper term and its capitalization variants; inspect lowercase matches in context so ordinary adjectives are not mislabeled as people-names.
2. Inventory coined crew labels and descriptive creature/object names, then verify who coins each term and whether canon permits that scene as its introduction.
3. Search personal-name and address variants, including forbidden bare names and family nicknames, against relationship/register rules.

Report zero-hit gated terms as a compact list and give line-level evidence for any allowed coined term. This catches premature knowledge and naming-register errors even when there is no constructed-language dialogue to parse.

## Reporting

Use one finding per error class. List all prose and synchronized-doc occurrences together, state the canonical form and meaning, and say whether the issue is spelling, grammar, semantics, formatting, glossing, or gating. If all checks pass, say which term was reviewed and why its reveal is permitted; do not report a vague `conlang clean` conclusion without the gate evidence.
